¶ Key terms
▸
Citation, byline, boilerplate, fact sheet, embargo, and schema, in plain English.

Citation (or quote)

a journalist quotes you as an expert source inside their own article. This is the most reliable form of earned coverage, and what we target most through reactive and proactive pitching.

Byline

an article published under your name. Your 3 authority articles are bylined on your own channels (site, LinkedIn, Medium) and guaranteed. A guest byline in a third-party outlet is one possible form of earned coverage, pursued as upside, not promised.

Boilerplate

the standard about-us paragraph at the foot of every press release: about 100 words on who you are, what you do, and your key facts, written once and reused everywhere.

Fact sheet

a one-page reference a journalist can grab fast: founding date, what you do, team size, milestones, location, and leadership, in scannable bullets.

Embargo

you share the story with outlets ahead of time, on the agreement that none publish until an agreed date and hour. The defining feature is timing, not exclusivity: several outlets can hold it and all go live when you announce. An exclusive is the opposite, one outlet, first.

Schema

structured data, invisible code that states your key facts (name, role, links) in a format Google and AI assistants read directly, which helps you show up in knowledge panels and AI answers.
