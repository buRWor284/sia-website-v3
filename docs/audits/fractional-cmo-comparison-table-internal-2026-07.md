## How to use this (read this first)

This table is a **call-prep and objection-handling aid**, not a script and not a public asset. Do not paste rows verbatim into emails or share the file with prospects — some of the language here (especially the "Honest gaps" section) is intentionally more blunt than anything Irfan would say to a buyer. Use it to know the landscape cold, then talk in Irfan's own voice on the call.

### Talking points by objection

**"Why not just hire a full-time CMO?"**
Lead with the math, not the pitch: a full-time CMO runs $300K+ loaded before a single campaign ships, and that hire still needs an execution team (designers, SEO, PR, content) layered on top at extra cost. Irfan's retainer ($5K–$10K/mo) comes with DMR.agency's execution built in — so the effective comparison isn't "$8K/mo vs. $25K/mo salary," it's "$8K/mo fully staffed vs. $25K+/mo salary plus a second budget for execution." Close with the fit filter already on the page: this is for founders who need senior thinking now, not a full org chart yet.

**"Why you, and not a bigger agency or a fractional-CMO firm like Chief Outsiders / Kalungi / CMOx?"**
Don't compete on their turf (roster size, brand-name enterprise clients). Compete on specialization: most fractional-CMO firms and marketplaces are generalists who staff whoever's available — you get a rotating or unknown operator. Irfan is a single specialist in earned media / digital PR / GEO-AI-search citability, a niche almost no fractional CMO on the market (see table) actually owns. If the prospect's growth lever is content, PR, or organic/AI-search visibility — not paid acquisition — that specialization is the differentiator, not scale.

**"Why should I trust proof that's agency results, not CMO results?"**
Don't dodge it — Irfan's own strategy is to own this directly (see audit action #1). Say plainly: "I've never held the CMO title, and this offering is new, so what you're seeing is the same team and system that will run inside your retainer, proven on other clients' revenue and traffic — not a personal leadership track record yet." Then pivot to what *is* verifiable and rare: World Bank keynote, SEMrush-commissioned workshop, Forbes/HBR/Entrepreneur bylines, 39-episode podcast. That's real authority-signal proof, just not the CMO-title kind. The $3,000 Marketing Leadership Audit exists precisely to let a skeptical buyer test this at low commitment before six months.

**"What if we already have an agency or in-house team?"**
This is where the execution-team differentiator cuts the other way — position Irfan as the strategy layer that directs existing resources, not a replacement for them. Most solo fractional CMOs in the table can only advise; Irfan can both direct strategy and, through DMR.agency, actually staff the gaps if the existing team can't cover PR/SEO/content.

**"Can we start smaller before six months?"**
Yes — the $3,000 Marketing Leadership Audit (2 weeks, credits 100% against month one) is the answer. Most competitors in the table (see "Entry point" column) offer only a sales call as the first step; Irfan's audit is a genuine paid, deliverable-producing on-ramp. Use this any time the 6-month minimum causes hesitation.

### Reading the table for call prep

- **On price:** Irfan's $5K–$10K/mo sits mid-pack — above SaaS Consult's low tier ($5K) and below Geisheker's ($8K–$15K), in line with CMOx's stated range ($3K–$15K). Nobody in the table is meaningfully cheaper *and* agency-backed; the closest price-and-model comparison is Geisheker (agency, $8K–$15K, no testimonials at all) and PHA Group (agency-backed, no pricing, weak proof).
- **On execution team:** only 4 of 13 are clearly agency/team-backed (Geisheker, Kalungi, PHA Group, and Irfan). The rest are solo operators or marketplaces of solo operators. This is the single cleanest structural differentiator to lead with against Demand Revenue, Source Approach, SaaS Consult, Growth.cx, and CMOx.
- **On specialization:** nobody else in the table specializes in earned media / digital PR / AI-search citability specifically. Source Approach specializes in eCommerce operations; Demand Revenue in PE-backed B2B SaaS; Geisheker in B2B/SaaS revenue metrics generally. Irfan's niche is a channel specialization that cuts across verticals — differentiate on *what* is being led, not just *who* it's for.
- **On entry point:** Irfan's $3,000 audit is the only paid, deliverable-producing first step in the table. Everyone else's "smaller step" is a free call. This is a genuine, ownable advantage for risk-averse buyers — use it whenever the 6-month minimum is the objection.

## Honest gaps (say this to yourself before every call, don't paper over it)

Irfan's stated strategy is honesty over proof-inflation. In that spirit, here is where this comparison is genuinely weaker for him, not just where it's strong:

1. **No formal CMO title, ever.** Demand Revenue's "11-time CMO" and Kalungi's CMO-tenure testimonials are a real credibility asset Irfan cannot match or fake. The FAQ on the live page already addresses this directly ("Have you personally held the title of CMO before? No..."), and that's the right move — but it means a buyer who is specifically screening for "has led a marketing org before" will correctly rule Irfan out. Don't try to talk around this; the fit filter exists to pre-qualify past it.

2. **No fractional-CMO client testimonials yet.** Every testimonial on the page is DMR.agency execution work (SEO, digital PR, earned placements), not a quote about the retainer/leadership engagement itself. This is exactly why the audit capped "proof" at 4/5 instead of 5/5, and it's the most durable gap — it only closes when the first real fractional-CMO engagement produces a client willing to speak to strategy ownership and cadence, not just campaign results. Landing that first client and testimonial remains the single highest-leverage fix (per the audit's action #2), above anything a comparison table or page edit can do.

3. **Newest offering in a field with established players.** Source Approach, Demand Revenue, and Geisheker have iterated their pages and offer over multiple cycles; Irfan's fractional CMO service is new (2 seats, Q3 2026 cohort). Being new isn't disqualifying, but it means no track record of multi-year retainer renewals to point to yet — something a buyer may ask about directly.

4. **Smaller scale than the marketplace/network competitors.** CMOx and Chief Outsiders operate a roster/marketplace model with many practitioners and broader category coverage. Irfan is a single specialist — a strength for buyers who want one dedicated operator, but it does mean no bench depth if the engagement needs a completely different specialization (e.g., pure paid-media leadership) than earned media/PR/GEO.

**Bottom line for calls:** lead with agency-backed execution + earned-media specialization + the audit as a low-risk entry point. Don't lead with, or imply, CMO tenure. If a prospect's core requirement is "has been a CMO before" or "show me a fractional-CMO client reference," say so plainly and let the fit filter do its job — that's a "probably not if" case, not one to spin.
